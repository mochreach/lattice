Lattice is a feature-rich audio tracker that enables you to make music in your terminal.
It is driven entirely through your computer's keyboard with key bindings
modelled on the [Helix](https://helix-editor.com) text editor.
It has a custom-built sample-accurate sequencer and sound engine that are
highly performant. It contains everything you need to go and make music, enabling you
to get your musical ideas out of your head as quickly as possible.

## Why Lattice exists

I've always liked the idea of a tracker; they're intuitive and precise, with a wonderful
complexity bubbling below the surface. I have used both software and hardware trackers,
spending a lot of time with both Renoise and the Polyend Tracker. Unfortunately, I've
always come away with a sense of cognitive dissonance, as I would consistently come
across implementation details that defied my expectations. Now, this is just a "skill
issue", right? Well, definitely: if I had continued to spend time working with them,
I would have got to a point where it felt natural to use them. However, in my mind, there
was the shadow of a piece of software that would match my expectations and enable me to
enter a state of tracker-induced creative bliss. I wrenched this idea out from deep in my
id, and thus, Lattice was born.

The intended audience for Lattice is me: every feature and tool has been created to
satisfy some, potentially unreasonable and fleeting, creative desire. All I care about is
translating my musical ideas out of my head and into the world. That's it. Every choice
serves that, so I've been uncompromising about how easy it is for anyone
else to use. All the same, I think it's a fascinating piece of software, and an interesting
way to make music. The documentation is very comprehensive; there is a tutor mode, and it's
made to explain what options are available and what they do, so it is quite usable. I hope
other people get something from it and enjoy it, and I'm curious to see how
they use it. But that's the philosophy: Lattice is made for me first.

An interesting aside here: I have made Lattice with the diligent assistance of AI. Most of
the code has been written via an LLM. **I could not have made this before agentic programming.**
Software this complex, feature-rich and performant, written for an audience of one person,
would have been a preposterous notion until very recently. Now, not only is it possible,
but it's accessible to most people. I think that presents an exciting and important
opportunity: go and make software that works for you.

I'm Chris Wells Wood, and if you'd like to hear more from me, I make videos on my YouTube
channel, [Mo chreach!](https://www.youtube.com/@mochreach), and write at
[mochreach.dev](https://mochreach.dev).

## The manual

Everything Lattice does is explained in [the manual](docs/manual), with every key and command,
drawings of the screen and the reasons behind them. It's built into the app too: `:manual` opens
it, and `:manual TOPIC` goes to what it says about a topic. To learn the keys by using them, start
with the tutor: `lattice --tutor`, or `:tutor` from inside. `:help` lists every key and command
as they're bound.

## Building from source

Lattice is pre-alpha, and it's built and run on Linux. You need a Rust toolchain recent enough for
the 2024 edition, `pkg-config`, and ALSA's development files (`alsa-lib`, or `libasound2-dev` on
Debian and Ubuntu). It plays through PulseAudio, which PipeWire also serves, or ALSA without it.

With Nix, `nix develop` gives you all of that. Then:

```sh
cargo build --release
target/release/lattice                 # a demo song
target/release/lattice song.lattice    # open a song, or start a new one there
target/release/lattice --tutor         # the tutor
```

To put `lattice` on your path, `cargo install --path crates/lattice-tui`. The other options
(`--no-splash`, `--no-click`, `--config`, …) are in
[Starting Lattice](docs/manual/01-introduction.md#starting-lattice).

## Examples

- [`examples/amen`](examples/amen): the Amen break programmed on the drums, sampled, and chopped
  up a step at a time.
- [`examples/mountain-king`](examples/mountain-king): Grieg's *In the Hall of the Mountain King*,
  using most of what Lattice does in one song.
- [`examples/scarlatti`](examples/scarlatti): Switched-On Scarlatti, his Sonata in G, K. 455, on
  Krell, the alien harpsichord, with a modular screaming along over a drum machine.

Open one with `lattice examples/scarlatti/scarlatti.lattice`, then `C-space` to play the song.

## How it's made

| Crate            | Role                                                                        |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `lattice-core`   | Song data, the rules that transform patterns, `realise()`. No UI or audio. |
| `lattice-engine` | The sequencer's clock, voices, DSP and mixer. Real-time safe.               |
| `lattice-editor` | The modal editor's state and keymaps, independent of the frontend.          |
| `lattice-tui`    | The terminal frontend: the `lattice` binary.                                |

The audio thread never allocates, locks or waits, and debug builds abort if it allocates, so a
real-time mistake shows up at once. Outside tests the code can't panic: the workspace's lints deny
`unwrap`, `expect` and indexing, since a panic would take the song with it. Scripts and agents can
drive a running Lattice through `lattice ctl`
([Driving Lattice from scripts](docs/manual/10-files-and-config.md#driving-lattice-from-scripts-lattice-ctl)).

The design, and what was learned from a first attempt as a GUI
([mochreach/lattice-legacy](https://github.com/mochreach/lattice-legacy), archived), are in
[docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md). How to work on the code is in [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).
